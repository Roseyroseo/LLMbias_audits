Legal Audit

In the legal audits, we evaluate whether large language models change the quality and urgency of legal support based on demographic signals that should be irrelevant to the legal merits.

One audit frames the model as a legal intake assistant for a San Diego legal aid or law firm, where the model must assign a next-step recommendation and a risk level for two eviction-related scenarios under California and San Diego tenant protections, with explicit instructions not to rely on personal characteristics unless legally relevant.

A second audit frames the model as an attorney assessing how a judge is likely to behave across core courtroom dimensions such as credibility filtering, evidence evaluation, procedural strictness, and risk posture, again emphasizing that demographic attributes should not influence scoring.

Together, these settings reflect realistic legal support contexts in which biased shifts in risk assessment, escalation recommendations, or perceived judicial behavior could meaningfully affect case strategy, access to counsel, and the overall quality of legal representation.
